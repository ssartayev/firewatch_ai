HKTech300, 21st Cohort · 5 minutes · two speakers. Total scripted time 4:44, leaving 16 seconds of slack. If you are running late, cut the Competition slide — it is the only one the deck survives without.

BEKARYS — the why

Opens and closes. Owns the story, the market and the money. Nine slides.

SANZHAR — the how

Owns the demo and the technology. Six slides, including the two that carry video.

Before you open your mouth

This deck opens on a disaster that killed 168 people ten months ago, in this city. Delivered plainly it is the strongest opening in the room. Delivered with any theatricality it will offend people who lost someone. Do not perform it. State it.

And never claim your product would have saved them. The alarms failing was a maintenance and compliance failure. Your claim is narrower and true: nobody could say who was inside.

The run sheet

FromToSlideWhat to say
0:00BEKARYSCover — Who is still inside?
Two beats of silence. Then: “On the 26th of November last year, in Tai Po, nobody could answer this question.”
Do not rush the silence. It buys you the room.
0:10BEKARYS168
“One hundred and sixty-eight people died at Wang Fuk Court. Forty minutes from this room. It was Hong Kong’s deadliest fire since 1948.”
Say the number slowly, once. Do not add adjectives — the number is the adjective.
0:28BEKARYSThree failures
“Three things failed. The alarms — not one of the eight towers sounded. The information — 467 missing-person reports, because nobody could say who was inside. And the escape — most of the dead were found inside their own flats.”
CRITICAL: then say “we cannot fix an alarm nobody maintained.” Never imply your product would have saved them. Judges will respect the restraint.
0:50BEKARYSMost vertical city
“Hong Kong has 570 towers over 150 metres — more than Shenzhen, more than New York. Nine thousand high-rises in total. Nowhere on earth stacks more people vertically.”
One sentence per bar. Do not read the whole chart.
1:05BEKARYSPeople do not run
“And here is what fire engineers know and the public does not. People do not run. The median delay before anyone in the World Trade Center even started moving was three minutes. Then they walk to the staircase they came in by — so one jams and two stand empty.”
This is your credibility moment. Then hand over: “Sanzhar will show you what we built.”
1:27SANZHARWhat we built — live demo
“This is our system running. 84 people, three staircases. Watch the top line.” Wait for the red bar. “There. It just noticed people are not going where they were sent, and it is rewriting the plan. It did that 74 times in three and a half minutes.”
THE MONEY MOMENT. Stop talking and let them watch for three seconds before you explain. Everyone else stops at detection; this argues back.
1:59SANZHARTwo sensors
“Cameras count people until smoke blinds them. Radar keeps counting through smoke. On the right is our own footage, run through the real model — seven people, and those boxes are its actual output.”
Do not say ‘sensor fusion’. If asked for depth: a blinded camera can only miss people, never invent them, so we use its count as a floor, never an average.
2:25SANZHARWhat gets installed
“Per floor: one camera, one radar sensor, speakers. The smoke and heat detectors are already in the building — we read the existing panel instead of replacing it. That is what makes a retrofit realistic.”
This slide justifies the per-floor price later. Keep it short.
2:41SANZHARFirefighter view
“That night there were 467 missing-person reports and 600 people searching. This is the alternative. Room 314 — eight people, camera blind, radar holding the count — and a way in that avoids the crowd coming out.”
End with: “It gives information, not orders.” Then hand back to Bekarys.
3:03BEKARYSMarket
“A thousand Hong Kong buildings are tall enough to need this. At HK$120,000 a building a year, that is a HK$120 million market. We are targeting the 570 towers that already employ fire-safety staff, and 3% of those by year three.”
Say the arithmetic out loud. Judges forgive an estimate they can follow.
3:23BEKARYSCompetition
“Alarm panels know where the fire is. Occupancy sensors know where people are — on a Monday, for desk planning. Nobody is in this corner.”
Point at the empty top-right quadrant. That gesture does more than the words.
3:39BEKARYSBusiness model
“Two ways in. Direct to owners: HK$22,000 a floor to fit out, HK$120,000 a year after. Or we license to the fire-safety firms who already hold the customer, at HK$45,000 a building. We have no revenue yet — we are testing which one works.”
Say ‘no revenue yet’ before a judge says it for you. It reads as honesty, not weakness.
3:59SANZHARTeam
“Four of us. I wrote the routing and fusion engine. Bekarys runs the company, Yerdos the numbers, Rauan the pilots. None of us is a certified fire engineer — that is our first hire, because we will not guess at life safety.”
Do not read the cards. The last sentence is the one that lands.
4:14BEKARYSThree years
“Year one: a fire-safety advisor and two pilot buildings. Year two: ten buildings and one installer reselling. Year three: fifteen to twenty buildings and a certification pathway.”
If pushed on revenue: this is arithmetic from our price, not a forecast.
4:32BEKARYSClose
“Fire safety has spent a century getting better at finding fire. We think the next one is about finding the people.”
Stop talking. Let the clip run behind you. Do not add a thank-you slide.

Handovers

3:03 — Sanzhar to Bekarys. “…it gives information, not orders.” Bekarys picks up on Market without a pause.

Questions you will get

QuestionAnswer
Would this have saved Tai Po?No, and we will not claim it. The alarms never sounded — that is a maintenance failure we cannot fix. What we address is the second failure: nobody could say who was inside.
Do you have customers?No revenue and no signed pilot yet. We have a working system and a price we intend to test. Year one is about finding the first building.
Is this certified?No. It is a decision-support prototype with no regulatory approval, and the deck says so. Certification is a year-three milestone, and our first hire is a fire-safety advisor.
Can radar really see people?It detects motion and micro-motion through smoke. It is not a people counter and we never treat it as exact — that is why we publish a range and a confidence.
Why would a building switch?They do not switch. We sit on top of the alarm panel they already own and read it. That is why the retrofit is HK$22k a floor and not a rip-out.
What stops Honeywell doing this?Nothing, eventually. Our lead is the occupancy model — the confidence-aware fusion and the reallocation loop — which is the part we wrote ourselves and keep improving.
