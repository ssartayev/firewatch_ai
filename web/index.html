SENSE AI understands the emergency—and the people inside it.

A normal alarm tells people there is a fire and points at the nearest exit. It has no idea how many people are in the building, where they are, or that everyone is about to pile into the same staircase. SENSE AI keeps track of all of that and splits the crowd between the exits so the building empties faster.

The loop runs continuously: sense, estimate, plan, guide, observe, compare, recalculate. A plan is a snapshot, never a commitment.

1 · It counts people

Cameras count who is in each room. When smoke blinds them, radar sensors that see through smoke take over. The system always says how sure it is.

2 · It shares out the exits

Instead of sending everyone to the nearest door, it works out how long each route will really take once it is crowded, and splits people between the three staircases.

3 · It checks and corrects

It watches where people actually go. When they ignore the instructions — and they do — it notices and works out a new plan.

Smart alarms that spot a fire and change the exit signs already exist. What is new here is putting people into the picture. How it works explains the engineering, and says plainly what this prototype cannot do.

SENSE AI is a working demonstration, not a finished product. The building, the people and the sensors in it are simulated. It is not an approved fire-safety system, it has no safety certification, and it cannot promise that a fire will be detected or that everyone will get out. Every people-count you see is an estimate with a stated confidence, never an exact count. The escape routes are a simulation on a simplified map of a building. The camera model is experimental, and any accuracy figure quoted comes from the people who published that model, not from us.
