Who is inside

Know where people are.

A camera counts people well — until smoke fills the room, and then it sees nothing. A radar sensor sees straight through smoke, but it can never give an exact number. So we use both, and each one tells us how sure it is. When they are not sure enough between them, we say “4 to 6 people” instead of pretending to know it is 5.

Camera vs radar — what each one is good at
Camera

Counts accurately when the air is clear. In smoke it does not just get unsure — it stops seeing people at all. A blinded camera can only ever miss people, never invent them, so we treat its count as a minimum: it can push our estimate up, never down.

Radar

Radar or heat sensing. It picks up movement — even breathing — straight through smoke. But it is not a counter: two people standing close together can look like one, and furniture confuses it. It does not see through walls, and we never treat its number as exact.

For engineers — the fusion maths

Each source's weight is its precision — conf² / (1 − conf), the reciprocal of its variance. Fused confidence is noisy-OR across independent sources. Below 80% the estimate is published as a range, and the planner routes for the top of that range rather than the middle.
